How to use this page

  1. Open Copilot at m365.cloud.microsoft, in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app, or in Teams, and keep this page open beside it. Keep the Work tab selected so Copilot can use your mail, chats, meetings and files.
  2. Have your own work ready: a recent email thread, a meeting you attended, a document and a spreadsheet you own. You will practice on your own work.
  3. Click Copy on any prompt, paste it into Copilot, and replace anything in [brackets].
  4. Two lanes. Lane A is the instructor's demo on fictional sample files. Just watch. Lane B is your turn: run the prompt on your own work, on your own screen, where nobody else can see it.

Can't find Researcher or Analyst? Look under Agents in the left pane of Copilot. If they are not there, run the same prompt in Copilot Chat. The answer is less detailed, but the lesson is the same.

The Copilot experience0:05 – 0:15

Orientation · 10 min · 4 units

Know where you are before you type

Runs on: Copilot Chat · Covers: Work and Web, Auto and model choice, agents, the four-part prompt, responsible use

Why this matters. Most disappointing Copilot answers come from the wrong setting, not a bad tool. Spend ten minutes here and the rest of the session goes faster.

Copilot ChatWork / WebAuto · Quick response · Think deeper
  1. Unit 1

    Work or Web: what can Copilot see?

    3 min

    Work grounds answers in your email, chats, meetings and files, always within the permissions you already have. Web uses public web content only. Same question, different answers. Try both.

    Work tab · paste this
    What are the three most important things I need to follow up on this week, based on my email, Teams chats and meetings? For each, tell me who it involves, what they are waiting on, and link the source.
    Switch to Web · paste this
    What are the top three trends affecting U.S. residential plumbing and kitchen fixtures this year? Cite your sources.
  2. Unit 2

    Auto, or choose the model yourself

    3 min

    Leave the model picker on Auto and Copilot chooses for you. Choose Quick response when you want a fast draft or a simple answer. Choose Think deeper for multi-step reasoning, trade-offs or tricky analysis. It takes longer and shows its working. The names in the picker can vary as Microsoft updates Copilot.

    Run once on Quick response, then again on Think deeper. Compare the answers.
    I lead a team that supports [your function] at Fortune Brands. We need to decide whether to [decision, for example: consolidate two weekly status meetings into one].
Lay out the options, the trade-offs for each, what could go wrong, and give me a recommendation with your reasoning.
  3. Unit 3

    The four-part prompt

    2 min
    PartAsk yourselfExample
    GoalWhat do I want?"Draft a one-page update…"
    ContextWho is it for, and why?"…for my VP, who has five minutes before the business review…"
    SourceWhat should it use?"…using the Q3 memo and last week's leadership meeting…"
    ExpectationsFormat, length, tone?"…three bullets, numbers first, plain language."
  4. Unit 4

    Review before you rely

    2 min
    1Check the numbers against the source.
    2Open the citations, especially on anything you will forward.
    3Ask "What did you leave out or could not verify?"
    4You own the output. Copilot drafts, you decide.

    Copilot uses the same permissions and protections as the rest of Microsoft 365. Your prompts and responses are covered by enterprise data protection and are not used to train the foundation models. Follow Fortune Brands policy on what you share.

Area 1 · Explore the market0:15 – 0:30

Area 1 of 4 · 15 min · 3 units

Category intelligence you can defend in front of a customer or partner

Runs on: Researcher · Covers: market and competitor research with sources

Use case. You have a meeting coming up with a customer, channel partner or supplier. They will ask what is changing in the market and how we compare. The information is public but scattered, and you have twenty minutes.

ResearcherDemo: Moen kitchen and smart water
  1. Unit 1

    Watch: a category review with sources you can click

    5 min
    Lane A · Instructor demoThe instructor prepares for a meeting with Harbor Plumbing Supply, a fictional wholesaler, about Moen kitchen faucets and smart water leak detection.

    Researcher breaks a complex question into steps, reads many sources, and returns a structured report with citations you can open, plus a list of what it could not verify. Why Researcher and not Chat: Chat gives you a fast answer. Researcher plans a multi-step investigation across the web and your work content, and gives you something you can defend when someone pushes back.

    Instructor prompt (Lane A)
    I am meeting Harbor Plumbing Supply, a regional plumbing wholesaler, next week about Moen kitchen faucets and smart water leak detection.
Give me three things changing in the U.S. kitchen faucet and smart water leak detection categories that would change how we price or position Moen over the next twelve months.
Name the competitors that matter most and how each one is positioned.
Cite every source with its date. Tell me plainly what you could not verify.
  2. Unit 2

    Your turn: your market, your next meeting

    6 min
    Lane B · Your turn, on your own workPick a real upcoming meeting and your own brand or category: Moen, House of Rohl, Therma-Tru, Fiberon, Larson, Master Lock, SentrySafe, Yale, or the area you support.
    Open the Researcher agent · paste this prompt
    I am meeting [customer, partner or supplier] about [product, brand or category] on [date].
Give me three things changing in the [category] market that would affect how we price, position or sell [our brand] over the next twelve months.
Name the competitors that matter most and how each one is positioned. Use my recent emails and files about [customer, partner or supplier] for context.
Cite every source with its date. Tell me plainly what you could not verify.
  3. Unit 3

    Challenge: make it refuse to guess

    4 min
    Challenge · stay in the same conversation and paste this
    Now use only sources published in the last eighteen months. If something is not in those sources, say "not in the source" rather than estimating it.
Then tell me which of your three findings is the weakest, and why.
Finally, give me the five questions [customer, partner or supplier] is most likely to ask me, with a one-line answer for each.
    Takeaway. The value is not the summary. It is the citations, the dates, and the list of what could not be verified.

Area 2 · Turn data into a decision0:30 – 0:45

Area 2 of 4 · 15 min · 3 units

What your numbers are actually telling you

Runs on: Analyst · Copilot in Excel · Covers: data analysis, finding what a summary hides

Use case. A headline number looks good. Before you repeat it in a review, you want to know what is driving it and which parts are quietly going backwards.

AnalystCopilot in ExcelDemo: Moen Q3 FY26 vs Q3 FY25
  1. Unit 1

    Watch: from 1,920 rows to one sentence

    5 min
    Lane A · Instructor demoThe instructor uses FBIN Moen Kitchen & Smart Water Performance SAMPLE.xlsx. Water revenue is up 7.6%, but how much of that is price and how much is volume?

    Analyst reads the whole workbook, writes and runs the calculation, and shows its working so you can check it. Copilot in Excel then turns the answer into a formula column, a PivotTable and a chart inside the file.

    Instructor prompt (Lane A) · Analyst
    Using the attached Moen Kitchen & Smart Water Performance workbook, compare Q3 FY26 (July to September 2026) with Q3 FY25.
1. Split the change in net revenue into price and volume, by product line.
2. Name every product line, channel and region where units went down, and quantify each.
3. Tell me what happened to gross margin %.
Show the calculation so I can check it, and list any data quality issues you find.
  2. Unit 2

    Your turn: a spreadsheet you own

    6 min
    Lane B · Your turn, on your own workUse any sales, cost, operations, quality or project spreadsheet you work with. Follow Fortune Brands policy for sensitive data. A sanitized copy is fine.
    Analyst · attach your file · paste this
    Using the attached [report or export], compare [this period] with [comparison period].
Tell me the three most important changes, quantify each one, and explain what is driving them.
Name anything that went backwards.
Show the calculation so I can check it, and list any data quality issues.
    Or in Excel · open your file · Copilot pane · paste this
    Add a column that calculates [metric, for example: margin % or variance to target] for each row. Then create a PivotTable of [measure] by [category] and [period], and a chart that compares [period A] with [period B].
  3. Unit 3

    Challenge: argue against the headline

    4 min
    Challenge · same conversation
    If I told my leadership team "[your headline, for example: sales grew 7% this quarter]", what is the strongest case that it is weaker than it looks?
Give me the one number I should lead with instead, and the two areas I should be most worried about.
    Takeaway. In the demo data, about two-thirds of the growth is price, not volume, and bath faucet units fell. A summary prompt would not have shown that. A decision prompt did.

Area 3 · Compress the document pile0:45 – 1:00

Area 3 of 4 · 15 min · 3 units

Several documents, one table you can act on

Runs on: Copilot Chat with files · Copilot in Word · Covers: summarizing, comparing documents, catching contradictions

Use case. A new requirement, such as a regulation, policy, contract or specification, has to be applied to a list of products, customers, suppliers or sites. You need to know what is affected and whether the documents agree with each other.

Copilot ChatCopilot in WordDemo: WEB-26 · 8 kitchen SKUs
  1. Unit 1

    Watch: three documents read together

    5 min
    Lane A · Instructor demoThe instructor uses the fictional WEB-26 bulletin, the spec sheets and the product register: FBIN Water Efficiency Bulletin WEB-26 Rev 4 SAMPLE.docx, FBIN Moen Kitchen Faucet Spec Sheets SAMPLE.docx and FBIN Moen Kitchen Product Register SAMPLE.xlsx.

    Copilot builds one product-by-product table. The interesting part is not the "affected" column. It is the products that cannot be determined and the places where two documents disagree.

    Instructor prompt (Lane A) · Copilot Chat
    Read the attached WEB-26 bulletin, the kitchen faucet spec sheets and the product register.
For each of the eight kitchen faucet SKUs, tell me whether it is affected by the new 1.5 gpm limit, not affected, or cannot be determined, and explain why. Cite the bulletin section you relied on.
Put it in a table with the Region W revenue for each SKU, and total the revenue that is affected.
Flag anywhere two documents disagree.
  2. Unit 2

    Your turn: a document you need to apply

    6 min
    Lane B · Your turn, on your own workUse a policy, contract, standard, specification or procedure you work with, plus the list it applies to if you have one.
    Copilot Chat · type / to reference your files, or attach them · paste this
    Read [/policy, contract or specification] and [/list, register or tracker].
For each [product, customer, supplier or site] in the list, tell me whether the requirement applies, does not apply, or cannot be determined, and explain why. Cite the section you relied on.
Put it in a table and flag anywhere the two documents disagree.
    Or in Word · open your document · Copilot · paste this
    Summarize this document in one page for [audience]: what changes, by when, what we must do, and who typically owns each step. Use plain language and a short table of key dates.
  3. Unit 3

    Challenge: close the gaps

    4 min
    Challenge · same conversation
    For every item you marked "cannot be determined", and every place where the documents disagree, tell me exactly what information would resolve it and which team would typically own it.
Then quote the wording that supports each conclusion.
    Takeaway. Copilot does the reading. You check the exceptions. In the demo, one product's register entry contradicts its own spec sheet, which is easy to miss deep in a long document.

Build a prompt worth reusing

The four moves from today. A prompt you write once and run every month is worth more than a clever one you use once.

MoveWhat it doesWorked example on Moen
The decisionName what is actually at stake, not just the topic."I am deciding whether to hold the Q4 Home Center promotion at 15% or go to 20%."
The evidenceTell Copilot what it may use and what to stay away from."Use only the Q3 performance workbook and the promotion email thread. If it is not in those, say 'not in the source'."
The adversaryAsk for the case against you."Give me the strongest argument against going to 20%, and the one fact that would change my mind."
The deliverableSay exactly what you want back."One page: position in two sentences, three evidence bullets with figures, the counter-case, one recommendation."
Before and after, on the same Moen data
Summarize the Q3 results for me.

Accurate, safe and not very useful. It tells you what is in the file, not what to do about it.

I have to answer one question in Friday's Q3 review: is the Water growth real?
Use only the Moen Kitchen & Smart Water Performance workbook and the Q3 business review memo.
Tell me what share of the growth came from price rather than volume, name every product line where units fell, and quantify each.
Then give me the strongest argument that I am reading too much into this, and the one fact that would change my mind.
Two-sentence position, three sourced evidence bullets, the counter-case, one recommendation.

Follow-up · optional

From four prompts to one task

Runs on: Copilot Cowork, when it is enabled for you

Not part of the live session, and not enabled for Fortune Brands at the time of training. Once you are comfortable with the four areas, this is the next step: one instruction that runs a multi-step task across your files, mail and the web and returns finished documents. Availability depends on licensing and tenant configuration.

Copilot CoworkWater Q3 review
  1. Example

    One instruction, four deliverables

    Prepare my Q3 Water review pack. Work through this end to end and give me finished files, not a chat answer.
1. Market: research the kitchen faucet and smart water leak detection categories with Moen as the focus brand. Cite sources with dates.
2. Numbers: from the Moen performance workbook, split Q3 growth into price and volume and list every line where units fell.
3. Compliance: from the WEB-26 bulletin, spec sheets and register, build a product-by-product impact table and flag disagreements.
4. Briefing: combine 1 to 3 with my Water mail and meetings from the last 30 days into a one-page brief and a five-slide deck.
Flag anything you could not source rather than filling the gap.

    Rule of thumb: if you would do it in one sitting, use Chat. If you would hand it to someone for an afternoon, that is a job for Cowork.

Questions during the session: post them in the Teams chat. The Microsoft account team is monitoring Q&A throughout, and anything we cannot answer live will come back to you in writing.
