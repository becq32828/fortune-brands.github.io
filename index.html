How to use this page

  1. Open Copilot Chat at m365.cloud.microsoft/chat, in the Microsoft 365 Copilot app, in Teams, or in Edge. Sign in with your Fortune Brands work account and look for the green shield, which means enterprise data protection is on.
  2. Two lanes. Lane A is the instructor's demo on fictional sample files. Just watch. Lane B is your turn: try the same kind of prompt on your own work. Follow Fortune Brands policy on what you upload.
  3. Click Copy on any prompt, paste it into Copilot Chat, and replace anything in [brackets].
  4. Don't stop at the first answer. Every exercise has a follow-up prompt. Refining the answer is the most important skill in this session.

This session is Copilot Chat only. Everything here works without the Microsoft 365 Copilot add-on license. Copilot Chat uses the web and the files you upload. It does not search your mailbox, chats or OneDrive automatically.

Module 1 · Generative AI and responsible AI0:00 – 0:10

Module 1 of 4 · 10 min

How it works, why it can be wrong, and your role

Covers: what generative AI is, Microsoft's six responsible AI principles, reviewing before you rely on it

Copilot predicts a likely answer from patterns. That makes it fast and fluent, and occasionally confidently wrong. Your job is to review its answers, not to trust them blindly.

Foundations
  1. Know

    Review before you rely

    1Inaccurate: check facts, numbers and citations.
    2Biased: look for one-sided wording or missing views.
    3Outdated: confirm dates and current details.
    4Off-target: make sure it answered what you asked.
  2. Try

    Ask Copilot to check itself

    Explain how a generative AI tool like you produces an answer, in five plain-language bullets for someone who has never used AI at work. Then list three ways your answers could be wrong and how I should check for each.

Module 3 · Writing effective prompts0:25 – 0:45

Module 3 of 4 · 20 min

Four elements, best practices, and making Copilot yours

Covers: Goal · Context · Source · Expectations, draft then refine, common mistakes, custom instructions

Most of the difference between a weak and a strong answer comes from the prompt. Include four elements, then refine the answer through conversation instead of starting over.

PromptingHarbor Plumbing Supply (fictional)
  1. Know

    Anatomy of a great prompt

    ElementAsk yourselfExample
    GoalWhat do I want?"Draft an email…"
    ContextWho is it for, and why?"…to our contact at Harbor Plumbing Supply, a key wholesaler, whose Moen order is late…"
    SourceWhat should it use?"…using the details below…"
    ExpectationsFormat, length, tone?"…under 120 words, apologetic but confident."
  2. Demo 1

    Draft, then refine

    Lane A · Instructor demoThe instructor drafts an email to a fictional wholesaler about a delayed Moen shipment.
    Step 1 · draft
    Draft an email to our contact at Harbor Plumbing Supply explaining that their Moen kitchen faucet shipment is delayed three days due to a carrier issue. Apologize, give the new delivery date of Friday, and offer a quick call.
    Step 2 · refine in the same chat
    Make it shorter and warmer. Add a short bulleted list with the order number [order number], the original date and the new delivery date. Close with my name and title.
    Step 3 · repurpose
    Now give me a version I could send as a Teams message instead: three sentences, friendly, no bullets.
    Lane B · Your turn, on your own workWrite a real email you need to send this week.
    Your turn · draft
    Draft an email to [name] at [company or team] about [situation]. Include [key facts, dates or numbers]. Keep it under [number] words, [tone, for example: friendly and confident], and end with [next step].
    Your turn · refine in the same chat
    Make it shorter and clearer, put the most important point first, and give me one alternative subject line.
  3. Compare

    Before and after

    Too vague
    Write something about our suppliers.
    Specific: goal, context, source and expectations
    I'm a buyer at a home products manufacturer preparing for a quarterly supplier review. Draft a one-page agenda for a 45-minute meeting with a key brass castings supplier whose on-time delivery dropped to 81%. Include discussion points, the data to bring, and three questions to ask. Use a professional, collaborative tone.
  4. Make it yours

    Set custom instructions

    Select … (Settings and more) › Settings › Personalization and tell Copilot about yourself once, so you don't repeat it in every prompt.

    Example custom instructions
    I work in [department] at Fortune Brands, a home and security products company (brands include Moen, Therma-Tru, Fiberon, Master Lock and Yale). I usually write for [audience]. Prefer plain language, short paragraphs and bullet points. Use U.S. English. When you are unsure, say so.

  4. Outlook

    Copilot Chat in Outlook

    In Outlook, select Copilot to summarize a thread you have open, draft a reply, or coach your writing. Copilot Chat works on the email you are viewing. It does not search your whole mailbox.

    Open a long thread · Copilot · paste this
    Summarize this email thread in five bullets and list anything I am being asked to do, with dates.
    Draft a reply
    Draft a reply confirming we accept the proposal and asking for the updated contract by [date]. Keep it brief and friendly.
  5. Create

    An image for a team newsletter

    Create a warm, photographic-style image of a modern kitchen at sunrise with a brushed-nickel faucet and a window view of a garden, for our team newsletter. Navy and gold accents. No text and no logos.

    Describe the subject, style, colors and mood. Ask for variations until it is right. Avoid real logos and real people.

  6. Share

    Pages and documents

    1Edit in Pages: select … under a response › Edit in Pages to keep it and edit it.
    2Build: add more responses, or type / for a table or checklist.
    3Share or export: share the page, or ask "Create a Word document of this."

Hands-on · From messy notes to a finished plan1:00 – 1:15

Five prompts in the same chat take you from rough notes to a brief, an agenda and an invitation.

Lane A · Instructor demoThe instructor plans the fictional Fortune Brands Pro Partner Summit 2027 from FBIN Pro Partner Summit Planning Notes SAMPLE.docx.
Lane B · Your turn, on your own workPlan something real: an event, a team offsite, a project kickoff, a training day or a customer visit. Paste your own notes, or start from a few bullets.
  1. Step 1

    Make sense of the notes

    3 min
    Lane B · paste this
    Read these notes for [event or project]. Summarize it in five bullets: purpose, audience, location, dates and budget. Then list every open question and every place where my notes contradict each other. [paste notes or upload the file]
  2. Step 2

    Research

    3 min
    What are current best practices for [type of event or project] for [audience]? Give me five ideas that would make it more valuable. Include sources.
  3. Step 3

    Build the agenda or plan

    3 min
    Draft a [length] agenda or plan using my notes plus your two best ideas from the research. Include times or dates, owners, and the goal of each item. Put it in a table.
  4. Step 4

    Write the brief

    3 min
    Write a one-page brief with goals, audience, agenda summary, a budget table that stays within [budget], risks, and the decisions still open. Then select Edit in Pages, or ask: Create a Word document of this brief.
  5. Step 5

    Invite people

    3 min
    Draft an invitation email for [audience]. Warm and professional, under 120 words, with three reasons to attend and a placeholder for the registration or meeting link.
    Bonus
    Create a clean, modern banner image for the invitation: [describe the scene], in navy and gold. No text and no logos.
